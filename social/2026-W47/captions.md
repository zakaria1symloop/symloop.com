## Day 1 — Tue 17 Nov 2026 — same-list

Images: `day1-same-list-en.jpg` (English) · `day1-same-list-ar.jpg` (Arabic)

### LinkedIn (English)

We sent two search engines 3,816 requests: phrases a buyer types, such as “software development company in…”, in four languages. Then we let them finish the sentence.

First result of our first study: a buyer in Riyadh, Dubai or Doha is handed the same list as a buyer in London or New York. On Google, the 1,309 letter-by-letter suggestions are identical in all five markets.

Search engines do not hand out one map per country. They hand out one per language.

A suggestion is a phrase an engine proposes. It is not a search volume and not a ranking of quality. But it is the first thing a buyer sees.

Method, limits and the full dataset are open:

## Day 2 — Wed 18 Nov 2026 — india-37

Images: `day2-india-37-en.jpg` (English) · `day2-india-37-ar.jpg` (Arabic)

### LinkedIn (English)

Type “software development company in” in English and let the engine finish. 37 % of the places it proposes are in India: Bangalore, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune, Noida, Ahmedabad.

The United States follows at 17 %. Then the UAE and Pakistan at 5 %, the UK and Qatar at 4 %.

This is the same whether you type from Riyadh, Dubai, Doha, London or New York. We checked with control markets: the largest gap on any country’s share is 0.2 point.

We do not know why. The simplest hypothesis is that the list reflects what is typed in English around the world. We did not measure that, so we call it a hypothesis.

What the map does not tell a buyer is who delivers.
https://symloop.com/blog/outsourcing-map-2027-what-search-engines-show-software-buyers-saudi-uae-qatar-france-spain/

## Day 3 — Thu 19 Nov 2026 — own-country

Images: `day3-own-country-en.jpg` (English) · `day3-own-country-ar.jpg` (Arabic)

### LinkedIn (English)

How local is search for a Gulf buyer?

In English, a buyer in Saudi Arabia finds Saudi Arabia in 2 % of the places search suggestions propose for a software partner. In the UAE it is 5 %. In Qatar, 4 %. For comparison, an American buyer finds the United States in 17 %.

The engine does localise, but only the first line, and not everywhere. Everything after it is the same global list.

For a Gulf company this has a practical meaning: the default suggestions will not surface the local market, and they will not surface most of the region either. The shortlist has to be built on something else.

### LinkedIn (English)

“But search is local.” It is, for exactly one line.

We looked at what Google proposes first when a buyer has typed the seed and a space. In Dubai the first suggestion is local on four English seeds of six. In Doha, three. In Riyadh, one: the other five start with India, Hyderabad, Kolkata or Malaysia.

Type one more letter and the list is everyone’s list again.

For a vendor, that means one local place per query, and a page that otherwise belongs to those the whole world already types. Visibility by default is not a strategy.

## Day 7 — Wed 25 Nov 2026 — five-proofs

Images: `day7-five-proofs-en.jpg` (English) · `day7-five-proofs-ar.jpg` (Arabic)

### LinkedIn (English)

After a week of numbers, the practical part.

A search suggestion tells you what many people typed. It does not tell you who delivers. Before you shortlist a country or a company because it appeared by itself, ask for five things you can check without believing anyone:

1. Reviews counted by a third party.
2. Named work, with a client you can call.
3. A published measurement, with its method, that anyone can re-run.
4. A live product you can open before signing.
5. A named team, and code ownership written in the contract.

Ask three vendors from three countries the same five questions. Including us.

The study, its limits and the open dataset:
